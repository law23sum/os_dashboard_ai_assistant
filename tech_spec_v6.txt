0.1 Mission, Product Scope & Strategic Objectives
0.1.1 Mission
The OS Dashboard AI Assistant (“OS Dashboard”, “the OS”) is a driver-aware AI operating system for knowledge and
execution work.
Its mission is to:
• Govern, automate, and augment the full lifecycle of high-value work across:
○ Documents, code, research, simulations, writing, finance, security, and operations.
• Turn real workflows into executable Capsules (repeatable workflows + environments) that can be run, audited, shared,
and improved over time.
• Provide a single, auditable control surface over:
○ OS and Unix execution, package & environment topologies,
○ SaaS and infra APIs, databases and research tools,
○ Policies, billing, compliance, and regulator views.
• Enable governed autonomy: AI agents can act on files, tools, systems, and infra—but only through governed drivers
and Capsules, under explicit policy and with full traceability.
The system must serve simultaneously as:
• A local polymath workstation (desktop + CLI) that behaves like a personal “Jarvis” on a single machine.
• An enterprise AI OS (multi-tenant backend + governance + marketplace) that can be adopted by teams, companies,
and regulators, with regulator-grade audit and policy enforcement.
• A foundation for cross-org / civilization-scale coordination through higher Meta-Stack layers (Advanced → Super →
Hyper → Ultra → God).

0.2 Design Principles, Constraints, Assumptions & Non-Goals
0.2.1 Core Design Principles
1. Auditable by construction
Every meaningful action (model call, driver call, Capsule run, environment change, policy decision) is recorded in the
Project Ledger and observable via Record Auditor and Evidence Packs. The system must always be able to answer:

“Who/what did what, when, under which policy, with which environment and inputs?”

2. Driver-first, not tool-first
All external capabilities (OS, Unix, package managers, SaaS APIs, DBs, lab tools) are exposed as drivers with
manifests, not opaque tools or ad-hoc shell access. Unix/Kernel execution and package managers are treated as
first-class, governed drivers, not side channels.
3. Plane separation (Data / Control / Governance)
Data motion (CIR, Ledger, indices), control decisions (plans, driver orchestration), and governance decisions (policies,
budgets, compliance) are logically separated, even when co-located physically. This enables reasoning, scaling, and
safety.
4. Local ≡ Enterprise (conceptually)
Local and Enterprise modes share the same conceptual architecture and domain model. Differences lie in scale, driver
scope, trust boundaries, and governance strictness—not in vocabulary or semantics.
5. Domain-neutral substrate, domain-rich surfaces
Core abstractions (Projects, Tasks, CIR, Capsules, Drivers, Policies, Twins) are domain-agnostic. Workspaces and
Engines compose these into domain-specific UX and behavior (Dev, Research, Writer, Cyber, Finance, etc.).
6. Governable automation
Automation is always subject to policy evaluation, budget checks, and safety harnesses—especially for System
Execution and Package & Environment drivers. There is no unguarded “root shell” for agents.
7. Self-improving over time
The platform continuously learns from execution traces, Capsule performance, Ledger history, and TRF/AIC insights to
refine:
○ Plans and strategies,
○ Environment specs and blueprints,
○ Capsule templates and packs,
○ Policies and guardrails.
8. Layered Meta-Stack with graceful degradation
Advanced/Super/Hyper/Ultra/God layer capabilities are explicit and optional. Each higher layer must degrade cleanly if
disabled, without corrupting the core.
9. Local-first optionality & offline resilience
Local Mode must be able to function with degraded or zero network connectivity and, where possible, with local or
cached models, deferring sync of ledger and evidence.
10. Explainable cognitive behavior
Personas and daemons must produce reasoning traces, references, and justifications suitable for human review, audit,

0.2.2 Constraints & Assumptions
• Runs on top of existing OSes
OS Dashboard is not a bare-metal kernel; it runs atop Windows, macOS, and Linux.
• Unreliable external systems
All external systems (APIs, CLIs, OS environments, SaaS) are assumed unreliable; the design must include retries,
backoffs, fallbacks, idempotency, and degraded modes.
• Heterogeneous estate
Must support mixed environments:
○ Windows/macOS/Linux, on-prem and cloud,
○ Modern SaaS, legacy UIs, air-gapped lab machines.
• Multi-model, evolving Model Layer
Multiple model providers and on-prem models will coexist, with evolving capabilities; the Model Layer is pluggable and
cost-aware.
• Regulated contexts
Enterprise Mode assumes regulator presence or future regulator interest; explainability, reproducibility, and data
residency are non-negotiable requirements in many deployments.
• Sandboxed execution
For risky workflows (system commands, package installs, simulations), containerization/virtualization and Safety
Harnesses must be available.
0.2.3 Non-Goals (for this spec / v1)
• Replacing every existing MDM/endpoint or SIEM system; instead, integrate with and augment them.
• Prescribing a single vendor for models, storage, or orchestration; these remain pluggable.
• Providing full UX design or visual styling specifications beyond what is needed to express architectural requirements
(personas, approvals, evidence views, etc.).
• Implementing a generic programming language or low-level VM beyond what Capsule and Policy representations
require.
• Serving as a general-purpose, consumer-grade chat assistant unrelated to projects, drivers, or Capsules.

0.7.2 Core Interaction Pattern
1. Intent capture
○ User or persona expresses a goal via a Workspace (e.g., “deploy this service”, “run this simulation”, “prepare
audit pack”).
2. Planning & reasoning
○ Personas (Sora, Aria, AIC variants) and daemons generate a plan:
§ Which Capsules and drivers to use,
§ Which environments or twins to target,
§ What preconditions and approvals are needed.
3. Governance evaluation
○ Policy Engine evaluates plan:
§ Identity, roles, data classification, budgets, risk thresholds.
○ May require human approvals or regulator-defined checks.
4. Execution
○ Orchestrator runs Capsules and drivers according to the approved plan.
○ System Execution and Package drivers are invoked as governed, logged operations.
5. Observation & learning
○ Outcomes recorded in Ledger, CIR, metrics.
○ Daemons mine traces to update:
§ Capsule templates, environment specs, plans, and policies.
6. Packaging & sharing
○ High-value workflows are generalized into Capsule Packs, My Stack Capsules, and Marketplace entries.
Critically, models never directly call OS or external resources; they only propose plans that are realized via drivers and
Capsules under policy control.

0.8.2 Routing, Optimization & Policy
• Routing decisions consider:
○ Data sensitivity and classification,
○ Policy restrictions (e.g., “no external provider for secret data”),
○ Cost budgets, current spend, and cost tradeoffs,
○ Latency and performance requirements,
○ Capability requirements (e.g., code-specialized models, vision, long context).
• Billing Optimizer Daemon collaborates with Policy Engine to:
○ Choose “good enough” cheaper models when appropriate.
○ Reserve premium models for high-impact or complex tasks.
○ Suggest adjustments to Capsule and plan design to reduce model spend.
• Model usage is:
○ Metered and logged into AI Billing & Usage Fabric.
○ Attributed to Projects, Workspaces, Capsules, personas, and tenants.
